About the group

We work on quantum foundations, quantum-information physics, algorithms and communication, quantum neural networks, and quantum artificial intelligence.

Overview ↘Research areas ↘Research and training ↘

Overview

The NJU Quantum Information Group starts from conceptual and structural questions in quantum mechanics, and studies physical foundations, computable models and verifiable tasks in quantum information processing. Our work connects quantum theory, algorithms and communication with quantum neural networks and AI.

We connect physical law, computational power and intelligent information processing through the ways quantum systems carry and acquire information.

Research areas

QF

Quantum foundations and quantum gravity

Quantum measurement, complementarity, uncertainty relations and correlations, together with information-complete quantum theory and quantum gravity.

QIP

Quantum information, algorithms and communication

Quantum operations, walks and networks, quantum computation, quantum key distribution and quantum communication.

QAI

Quantum neural networks and AI

Quantum neural computing, representation and classification, sparse coding and generative tasks, and conditions for verifiable quantum learning advantage.

Research and training

We begin with precise problem definitions and combine mathematical analysis, numerical simulation, quantum-information protocols and complexity comparisons. Our work emphasizes how quantum systems represent, transmit and process information, and seeks verifiable connections between theoretical structures and tasks in quantum computation, communication and learning.

Teaching and discussion span quantum mechanics, principles and applications of quantum information, and quantum physics and AI; course offerings follow the School's current schedule.
